<h3>Overview</h3>
      <p>PII, or Personally Identifiable Information, should be kept safe; Certainly not in the hands of criminals with ill intent. Sadly, all too often is this not the case. Data can be breached and subsequently leaked to the public, or sold for a profit to the highest bidder. Not only this, but the very companies you trust with your information may be unknowingly handing it over without a second thought, through methods I will cover. Data is a gold mine for the new ages, where corporations, governments, and criminals all want their piece of the pie. The lucrativeness of it has become astronomical as the possibilities for profiting skyrocket.</p>

      <h3>Breaches and Leaks</h3>
      <p>Data breaches and their leaks are the most well known culprit for PII being found in the wrong hands. A breach can be boiled down to data being pulled out from its place of storage or in transit. A few attacks that can end in a breach include SQLi (SQL injections), inside attacks, a number of social engineering schemes including phishing, API exposure, and more. Breaches' leaks or sales are generally the backbone for a multitude of other attacks and avenues for obtaining information. The mass amount of data is pooled among other leaks and can be cross referenced to map a person's emails, IPs, phone numbers, etc. According to the University of North Georgia, since 2013 there are 3,809,448 records stolen from breaches per day, 158,727 per hour, 2,645 per minute, and 44 every second.</p>

      <h3>White Pages</h3>
      <p>White Pages are a common tool utilized by criminals doing recognisance. Often, they lay behind a paywall or provide only basic info. Their information is usually collected via public records, whether it be voter registrations, public utilities, or phone records. This is publicly stated by WhitePages themselves. They advertise the ability to potentially locate one's numbers, relatives, criminal record, addresses, age, carrier, and much more.</p>

      <h3>OSINT Services</h3>
      <p>OSINT, or open source intelligence, is defined as the act of retrieving and analyzing public information. These are similar to white pages. Services in this area exist in both a legal and illegal manner. A lot of the highly regarded services are costly, or limited to government employees, usually verified through a government issued email such as the TLD "@fbi.gov". OSINT software is generally considered a better tool than White Pages for what it does. Services use a wide variety of sources for information, often including leaked data.</p>

      <h3>TLO</h3>
      <p>'The Last One' is the name of the tool used by qualified businesses, governments, and law enforcement. The precision, detail, and ease of use that comes from this tool is what makes it both incredibly useful for authorities and agencies, as well as desired by criminals. It is advertised as an "Advanced linking algorithm that filters through massive data repositories to deliver actionable information in seconds (TLO, 2024)." This tops both OSINT and White Pages. TLO lookups have become so common amongst criminal communities, that it has taken on its own verb form. I was able to locate multiple public groups on Telegram that had mentioned the topic. Users can be seen threatening others, exclaiming that they will TLO them, or offering the services for a fee. Criminals gain access to such a tool by compromising police or government emails and devices, or finding ways of creating their own. The most popular way being phishing someone with lookup capabilities.</p>

      <h3>EDR</h3>
      <p>An emergency data request is a form that qualified individuals can file to quickly retrieve data on a user. These exist on most major social media, streaming, and financial platforms. Authorization to use the portal for these requests is limited, being only available to law enforcement agencies. However, as already discussed the emails for these individuals can be compromised and sold. An EDR is the single fastest way to get accurate targeted data on an individual.
